Comment 01 — Starting points

A start bad enough to fail

So, there are good, medium, poor, while 3 of them all converges. Could we figure a case that the starting points is too bad, so that theh convergence is impossible or too slow? § Parameter Estimation via Panel Iterated Filtering

What changed

  • A fourth quality, extreme, joins good, medium and poor. Its log-scale jitter standard deviation is 1.8, against 0.08, 0.45 and 1.0 — roughly twice the poor displacement, which moves a typical parameter by about a factor of six. qmd:343
  • The surrounding prose does not predict what the extreme group will do. Whether it recovers is reported from the run.
  • start_quality_schedule now cycles four labels and requires at least four starts. qmd:382
  • Run-level start counts move from 3 / 9 / 18 to 4 / 12 / 20, so every level holds balanced groups — three of each at level 2. qmd:106
  • Both model sections use the same four groups, and the SIRJPF2 PIF/MPIF comparison still starts every chain from matched medium-quality vectors.

Why

  • Three groups that all converge demonstrate only that the search is robust to the displacements that were tried. They cannot show where the method stops working.
  • A group that fails under the same budget is what turns the comparison into evidence: it locates the boundary rather than asserting there isn't one.
  • The level-2 render bore this out. Good, medium and poor terminated at −500.83, −500.32 and −500.67 on average — indistinguishable. The best run came from a poor start.

A judgement call worth knowing about

This started at 2.5 and was reduced to 1.8 on review. The measurement model returns a fixed −150 for each violating observation, which floors the SRJF panel likelihood at −150 × 10 × 10 = −15,000. A start displaced far enough to violate every bound sits on a flat penalty surface where MIF has no gradient at all — so it would demonstrate the tutorial's own soft-constraint device, not the limits of panel iterated filtering. The displacement is now large enough to defeat the budget while normally staying evaluable.

It is still a chosen number, not a derived one, and it could land either side of the target: recoverable after all, or far enough out that every chain returns -inf and the group has no plottable trace.

The second case used to be the dangerous one, because a group with no finite likelihood simply disappears from the figure — which is precisely how a failed start would hide. The speed table is therefore built from the groups that were started, not the ones that produced curves, so an all--inf group appears as explicit NaN rows and is named in a printed line beneath the table.

Comment 02 — Figure 2

One axis, connected markers

Please unifiy the y-axis scale for two subfigures, and instead of bar plot, use line plot where x-axis is the mean, dot and lines represents each bucket's log lik § Diagnostic 1: Parameter Scaling Verification

What changed

  • Both panels now share one vertical axis (sharey=True).
  • Bars are replaced by a marker per unit joined by a line, with each panel's mean over finite units drawn as a dashed reference. qmd:1467
  • A -Inf unit is placed just below the lowest finite unit and labelled, rather than being allowed to set the axis limits.
  • The caption is rewritten to say what the shared axis does to the well-scaled panel.

Why

  • The old version drew bars measured from each panel's own mean on independent axes. Two quantities that differ by roughly 140 log units per unit were rendered at the same visual size, which made them look comparable.
  • On a shared axis the well-scaled profile compresses to a near-flat line at the top. That compression is the finding: the between-unit variation that matters under correct scaling is small next to the cost of getting the scaling wrong.
  • Connected markers are also what daphnia_tut_pypomp.qmd and the R tutorial already use for this figure, so the three documents now agree.
Comment 03 — Figure 4

Convergence speed, measured

Please figure out a way to show that the difference of speed of three cases (now 4 cases') convergence speed, and probably no need to show all points, just show each cases' smooth curve. § Diagnostic 3: MIF searches visualization

What changed

  • Individual chains and their scatter points are gone. Each quality group is drawn as one smoothed median curve with an interquartile band. qmd:1725, qmd:3107
  • The figure has two panels. The left is the log likelihood itself, over its full range, which is where a group on a separate track is visible at all. The right is the remaining distance to the best final median, on a logarithmic axis: there a constant rate of approach is a straight line whatever the starting distance, so the four slopes are directly comparable — which is what “difference of speed” means. plot_quality_curves, qmd:236
  • A single linear panel put the extreme group 1800 log units below the rest and flattened the three that converge into one line — satisfying Comment 1 at the cost of Comment 3. Merely zooming the second panel was not enough either: medium and poor start 500–700 log units apart, so on a linear zoom the good group is still flat against the top.
  • A new printed table gives the speed measure directly, timed against one target shared by every group: the best final median any group reached, less 10 log units. Every row then answers the same question — how many iterations until this group came within 10 log units of the best outcome? qmd:1754, qmd:3132
  • The PIF/MPIF comparison is timed against a single target spanning both methods, so the slower method cannot look faster by having less ground to make up.
  • Smoothing is a centred rolling mean over a window of about 15% of the iterations, with min_periods=1 so the curve reaches both ends. qmd:210
  • The existing PIF vs MPIF comparison now uses the same helpers and the same smoothing, so the two convergence figures are read the same way.

Why

  • With four groups and twelve chains the raw traces overplot, and the question asked is about groups, not about what any single chain did.
  • A number beats an eyeball. “Which curve rises first” is a judgement; “90% of the median gain by iteration 37” is comparable across groups, models and run levels.
  • A group whose median never rises has no such iteration. It reports NaN rather than an imputed value, which is the honest answer for a start that did not converge.
  • The first attempt normalised by each group's own gain, and that measure could not compare groups. A group starting near the maximum has almost no gain to make, so 90% of it is reached instantly or — if its median does not rise — never, meaning the fastest group could report NaN. A group with a large gain reaches most of it early and looks fast while finishing far below everyone else. The shared target removes both failure modes.

A bug this exposed

The PIF/MPIF speed statistic already existed, and in the completed level-2 render it printed NaN for both methods. Iteration 0 records the starting parameters before any likelihood has been evaluated, so its logLik is missing for every chain; taking the median over that iteration produced a NaN starting value, and the whole statistic silently collapsed. mif_trace_curves now drops iterations with no finite value before taking quantiles. qmd:181

Comment 04 — Numeric output

Four significant digits

Only show 4 significant number, sam apply for all in this tutorial. § Diagnostic 4: Monte Carlo Adjusted Profile

What changed

  • Four formatting helpers in the setup chunk: sf() for four significant digits, ll() for log likelihoods, interval() for a pair of endpoints, and table_text() which applies the right rule per column. qmd:132–180
  • Every print and every table in the document was swept. No to_string(float_format=…) or bare format specifier remains outside the helper itself.
  • Raw float and np.float64(…) reprs are gone from the MCAP summaries, which is where the sixteen-digit output you selected was coming from.
  • difference_from_float64 takes the four-significant-digit rule, not the log-likelihood one. It is a precision residual, and showing its magnitude is the whole point of that block; at two decimal places a small float32 error would print 0.00 and contradict the paragraph above it.
  • The document now states its numeric convention in the introduction, so a reader knows the rule without inferring it.

Why

  • The selected block printed values such as -0.3494616301710143; sixteen digits assert a precision the Monte Carlo estimate does not have.
  • Column-by-column dispatch, rather than one global float format, keeps a parameter value and its standard error on the rule that suits each.

Deviation — confirmed by the author

Applied literally, four significant digits turns a panel log likelihood of −498.954178 into −499.0. This document's central comparisons are differences of that quantity at the scale of its Monte Carlo standard error: the level-2 SRJF fit sits −0.17 from the stored reference with a combined MCSE of 0.46. At four significant digits that difference is not representable, and every reference and AIC comparison in the document loses its meaning.

So log likelihoods and their differences are given to two decimal places instead, and everything else — parameters, standard errors, coefficients of variation, timings, ratios — takes the four-significant-digit rule you asked for. Put to the author on 2 September 2026 and confirmed: log likelihoods stay at two decimal places.

Next run

Both precisions on one device

Set DEVICE_AVAILABLE to the free device index from nvidia-smi. The wrapper renders float64 first — that is the run that gets published — then starts a fresh process for the float32 diagnostic, and restores the float64 document afterwards, so the standard output is never the diagnostic. JAX fixes its precision when it initialises, which is why two processes are needed. Two renders of the same document must not run concurrently — they share DENO_DIR, TMPDIR and .quarto/. 

# pick a free device
nvidia-smi --query-gpu=index,memory.used,memory.total --format=csv

conda activate py313
cd ~/git/Daphnia-tutorial/Python-code

DEVICE_AVAILABLE=0
export CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES="$DEVICE_AVAILABLE"
export DAPHNIA_DOC=daphnia_tut_pypomp_advanced

# float32 diagnostic, then float64 standard, in separate processes
tmux new -d -s daphnia \
  "./render_gpu_precision_comparison.sh > daphnia_precision_comparison.log 2>&1"
tmux attach -t daphnia          # detach with ctrl-b d
